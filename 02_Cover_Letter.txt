Dear Editor,

I am writing to submit "CAGate: Cluster-Aware Gating for Differentiable Causal Discovery in Cancer Genomics" for consideration in Bioinformatics.

The idea came from a simple observation: methods like NOTEARS are great at learning causal graphs from data, but on real cancer transcriptomics they fall apart once you go beyond about 150 genes. That threshold cuts out most of the interesting biology. Nobody seemed to be asking whether the cluster structure that everyone knows exists in tumor data---molecular subtypes, immune states, copy-number-driven programs---could be used to fix this. So I tried building it directly into the optimization loop, and the results were stronger than I expected.

CAGate works by running adaptive clustering on model residuals at each outer iteration, then computing a MAD-aware gate weight per cluster. Homogeneous subpopulations drive edge discovery; noisy heterogeneous ones get down-weighted. There is no separate clustering step before causal discovery---the two co-evolve, which turns out to matter.

Across all 33 TCGA cancer types I tested (68 configurations total), CAGate consistently finds substantially more causal edges than NOTEARS: mean improvement of +158 edges, with the largest gains on small-sample cancers (+213 for n < 323). At d=200, CAGate recovers 552 edges where NOTEARS finds zero. The most striking result---one I only fully appreciated after benchmarking every cancer type---is that the method works best on the smallest datasets, precisely where you would expect it to fail. External validation against CTD (89-98%), ClinGen (69-82%), and STRING confirms the recovered edges are enriched for real biology, not noise.

I think Bioinformatics is the right home for this work because it is fundamentally a methods paper with large-scale biological validation: a lightweight extension to a known framework that opens up a previously inaccessible data regime. The method adds under 2% runtime overhead and requires no hyperparameter tuning beyond the single gating sensitivity parameter (the default worked on every cancer without adjustment). Complete code, TCGA download scripts, pre-computed results, and 10 figure-generation pipelines are at https://github.com/gsd3247186514-cell/CAGate-Replication. Every figure can be reproduced with a single command.
